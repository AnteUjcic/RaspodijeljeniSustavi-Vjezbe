What is barley?

Barley (Hordeum vulgare) is a cereal grain of the grass family, first domesticated in the Fertile Crescent around 10,000 years ago. It is one of the oldest cultivated crops and today ranks among the most widely grown grains in the world.

About a third of the global barley harvest is used for malting, mostly for beer and whisky. Brewers love barley because its husk acts as a natural filter during brewing, and its kernels are rich in starch and the enzymes needed to convert that starch into fermentable sugar.
